Calculator Methodology & Sources

How we build our insurance estimates — and where every calculation stops being a substitute for a licensed insurance professional.

Published by the FreeInsuranceIQ Editorial Team · Reviewed September 2026

What our calculators are designed to do

FreeInsuranceIQ calculators provide educational insurance estimates for planning and comparison purposes. They combine publicly available rate data, actuarial benchmarks, and user-entered values to produce illustrative results. These results are planning tools — not quotes, binding offers, underwriting decisions, or professional insurance advice.

Important: Insurance rates are highly individualized. Actual premiums depend on your specific profile, location, claims history, credit score (where permitted), and the insurer's current pricing. Always get quotes from licensed insurers before making coverage decisions.

How an estimate is built

  1. Published rate benchmarks: Rate ranges and averages are sourced from state insurance departments, NAIC data, and widely cited insurance publications (NerdWallet, Policygenius, Insurance Information Institute). These reflect market averages — not any individual insurer's pricing.
  2. Actuarial formulas: Life insurance need calculators use the DIME method (Debt, Income, Mortgage, Education) — the same framework financial planners use. ACA subsidy calculators use the federal Premium Tax Credit formula based on household income and FPL thresholds published by HHS.
  3. User-entered values only: Calculators apply only the values you enter. They cannot account for your claims history, specific location, credit profile, health status, or insurer-specific rating factors.
  4. Ranges, not quotes: Results are displayed as ranges because insurance pricing varies significantly by provider and individual risk profile. A single number would imply a precision the data does not support.
  5. Private calculation: All calculations run in your browser. No data is submitted to FreeInsuranceIQ or stored on our servers.

Authoritative sources we use

  • ACA subsidies and health insurance: HealthCare.gov, the KFF Subsidy Calculator, and HHS Federal Poverty Level tables published annually in the Federal Register.
  • Auto insurance rates: State insurance department rate filings, NAIC market share data, and the NHTSA for safety ratings that affect premiums.
  • Home and flood insurance: FEMA National Flood Insurance Program (NFIP) rate data and the Insurance Information Institute.
  • Life insurance benchmarks: LIMRA industry data, state insurance department filings, and published term life rate surveys from major comparison platforms.
  • Disability insurance: Social Security Administration disability statistics and Council for Disability Awareness research.
  • Finding licensed insurers: Your state's Department of Insurance (find via NAIC's state directory) to verify any insurer's license before purchasing coverage.

Limitations by calculator type

Life insurance calculators

The DIME formula provides a planning framework, not a binding recommendation. Actual coverage needs depend on your complete financial picture, existing assets, employer-provided coverage, and risk tolerance. A licensed life insurance agent or financial planner can provide personalized guidance.

Auto insurance calculator

Rate estimates are based on statewide averages. Actual premiums vary by ZIP code, vehicle, driving record, credit score (in states that permit it), and the specific insurer. Telematics programs, multi-policy discounts, and good-driver discounts are not modeled.

Health insurance subsidy calculator

ACA subsidy calculations use the current-year federal poverty level thresholds and benchmark premium data. Results may differ from your actual marketplace subsidy due to household composition details, state-specific programs, or mid-year income changes. Use the official HealthCare.gov calculator for enrollment decisions.

Home insurance calculator

Estimates are based on dwelling value and regional averages. Actual premiums depend on construction type, age, roof condition, claims history, proximity to fire stations, and insurer-specific underwriting criteria.
